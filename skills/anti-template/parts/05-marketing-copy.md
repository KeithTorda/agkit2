# 05 - Marketing copy and evidence

Read for pages meant to persuade, inform a purchase, invite enrollment, or build a brand. Use the voice and visual references selected in the intake. Marketing can be expressive, aspirational, funny, luxurious, technical, restrained, or bold. It does not have to sound like a settings screen.

## Tell a specific story

Choose the order from the audience's decision: what is offered, why it matters, what it looks or feels like, what supports the claim, and how to proceed. A hero may lead with a clear function or a distinctive emotional line. Its supporting copy and visual should establish enough context for the right visitor to understand the offer.

Headlines may be short or long, centered or aligned, multi-line or compact. Eyebrows, slogans, and CTA bands can be useful. Evaluate their role and composition rather than imposing quotas. Remove a section only when it adds no useful information or emotional purpose, not because the pattern is recognizable.

## Turn capabilities into meaningful benefits

Connect a feature with a concrete situation. "Reconcile a day's sales across all branches" gives a buyer more to assess than repeating "multi-branch dashboard." Examples and screenshots can demonstrate the product. A narrative or stylized image may communicate the brand where literal screenshots do not.

Use words that fit the brand. "Unlock your next chapter" may suit a campaign; a pricing promise still needs clear terms. Do not invent measurements merely to replace adjectives. Benefits can be qualitative when framed honestly.

## Evidence and trust

| Content | Required care |
|---|---|
| Testimonials and reviews | Use real, supplied or permissioned quotations; preserve meaning and attribution. Mark fictional prototype copy as fictional. |
| Customer logos and partner marks | Verify the relationship and right to use the marks; do not imply endorsement from a mere integration. |
| User counts, ratings, performance claims | Use a verifiable source and a meaningful period or basis. |
| Awards, accreditation, security badges | Use actual, current credentials with scope and source where relevant. |
| Product mockups and concept screens | Present them as concepts or previews when they do not represent a working product. |
| Team and About pages | Use real names, roles, story, and photos where supplied. Do not fabricate credentials or staff. |

There is no required number of testimonials, logos, benefits, or images. Absence of evidence is a reason to request real content or use a different section, not generate false proof.

## Pricing and conversion

Explain currency, billing interval, taxes or fees where relevant, limits, trial conditions, renewal, cancellation, and what changes between plans. Tier names and highlighted recommendations can be branded; a "most popular" claim needs actual support. Comparison tables should accurately represent both products and their current plans.

Use urgency only when a real deadline, inventory limit, or booking capacity exists. Countdowns must reflect that source. Do not reset a fake timer per visit. State the real terms of a guarantee; do not invent a legal promise.

Choose CTA wording from the next step and voice. "Get started" is fine for a clear onboarding path; "Book a fitting" is better when that is the action. Make reassurance useful and true. Newsletter signup needs an actual plan to send relevant mail and consent handling.

## FAQ, careers, and launch pages

FAQ should answer relevant objections or operational questions; no fixed count or required accordion. Careers pages must distinguish open roles from general inquiries. Planned features and launch dates should be marked as planned and based on real decisions. Contact details must be real before a public launch.

## Review

Read the promise, evidence, pricing, and CTA together. Confirm factual claims, consistency with the agreed voice, and the visitor's next step. Clearly flag content still needing client input. Preserve personality while removing deception and generic repetition.
