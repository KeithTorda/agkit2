# 24 - Page purpose and content structure

Read when planning a page type. Use these content considerations with the user's chosen style and references. They are not fixed section recipes. Familiar structures can be effective; do not force a hero, FAQ, testimonials, or a different layout in every section.

## Public and marketing pages

| Page | Useful decisions and content |
|---|---|
| Landing page | Audience, offer, chosen visual story, evidence, relevant detail, and real next step. The order follows the visitor's decision. |
| Pricing | Currency, interval, actual plan differences, limits, fees/taxes, trial/renewal, cancellation, and meaningful comparison. Cards, tables, calculators, or narrative can work. |
| About / team | Real story, people, roles, locations, and credible evidence. Do not fabricate founding history, awards, or staff. |
| Contact | Actual channels, hours, location where relevant, expected response, form recovery, and privacy expectations. A map is optional if useful. |
| Careers | Real roles and application process, or an honest absence of openings. Do not invent jobs to fill the design. |
| Portfolio | Selected work, context, role, process or result where useful, and permission to show it. Images and motion may lead when they serve the work. |
| Coming soon / waitlist | What is planned, who it serves, a real date if known, and an actual signup destination if collecting contacts. No fabricated countdown or roadmap. |
| Event | Real date/time zone, location or link, eligibility, accessibility details where available, tickets/registration, refund or cancellation information. |
| Link hub | Clear destinations, recognizable identity, and current contact/social links. No invented accounts. |

## Editorial and reference pages

An article page needs readable type, author/date where meaningful, clear structure, real links, and media context. A blog list should help selection through useful titles, descriptions, categories, or dates without a mandatory card layout. Search/filter behavior must reflect the actual indexed scope.

Documentation should prioritize finding an answer and completing a task. Show navigation, version, prerequisites, examples, and limitations as needed. A changelog should describe actual released changes and migration effects. Do not create a dead changelog solely to match a template.

Legal pages require real policy content appropriate to the organization and jurisdiction. Do not generate confident compliance promises or universal boilerplate as though approved counsel wrote it. Mark drafts and source unresolved requirements appropriately.

## Commerce and search

A product page should provide the actual item, images or representation, price, availability, variations, delivery/returns information, and clear purchasing action. Decorative lifestyle imagery can enrich the story; it should not replace information needed to buy.

Cart and checkout need accurate totals, currency, discounts, taxes/fees, delivery, payment state, validation, and duplicate-order prevention. Explain surprises before commitment. Pending gateway processing must not become a false success screen. Preserve entered information appropriately during failure.

Search results need query context, actual scope, result meaning, filters where useful, no-results, loading, and recovery. Avoid showing an empty result while the request is merely pending. Support return to results with useful state.

## Government, school, and civic pages

Structure around real services and audience tasks: requirements, schedules, fees where applicable, forms, locations, admissions, programs, or official notices. Branding may be expressive or formal according to the brief. A seal, official portrait, or red/blue palette is not automatically required.

Election and precinct tools need verified sources, freshness, privacy boundaries, and a clear distinction between official records and an unofficial aid. Never invent results, locations, officeholders, or eligibility rules. See part 09 for locale and authoritative-source considerations.

## Review

Read the page as a first-time visitor and as a returning user with a specific task. Check whether the content sequence, chosen composition, and real next action support both. Missing source content should be visible as an unresolved dependency or explicit prototype content, not hidden by fabricated proof.
