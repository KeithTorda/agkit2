# 08 - External messages and metadata

Read when writing messages outside the product or page metadata. Match the brand, channel, audience, language, and purpose. A social campaign can be expressive; a password reset must be clear and trustworthy.

## Transactional email and push

Identify the sender and event. A subject should help the recipient recognize the message and required action. Include relevant dates, amounts, references, expiry, or support routes without exposing sensitive information in lock-screen previews. Use the real product or organization name; a branded team name is fine when recognizable.

Verification and reset flows need a real destination, honest expiration, and advice for unexpected requests. Do not email passwords. Receipts should reflect confirmed transactions, currency, merchant details, and the applicable accounting information. Avoid fake payment confirmations.

Links must go to the intended environment and domain. Emails need readable fallback content and useful text when images fail. Test transactional templates in the delivery path when tools exist; a browser screenshot alone does not establish email-client support.

## SMS and short channels

State who is contacting the person, why, and what to do. Consider segment length, Unicode encoding, cost, delivery timing, and link readability. Emoji can change SMS encoding and segment count, so choose intentionally. Use actual provider and regional requirements for opt-out, consent, and sending rules; do not assume a fixed character budget or universal legal text.

Push notifications should be useful enough to interrupt. Respect preferences and avoid exposing private information. A notification's destination should open the relevant item, not an unrelated home screen.

## Social and public notices

Use the audience's natural register, including Taglish or regional language when appropriate. Hashtags, emoji, questions, and energetic punctuation are available tools, with no mandatory count. The message should still communicate the event, offer, date, location, eligibility, and real next step.

For official notices, verify the issuing office, source, effective date, and current status. Do not fabricate election details, deadlines, cancellations, emergency instructions, or officials. Distinguish an unofficial prototype from a published government source. Drafting content does not authorize sending or publishing it.

## Titles, descriptions, and sharing

Give important pages useful, distinct titles that describe their content and site identity. Length guidance is a preview consideration, not a character-count pass/fail test. A brand-led title can fit a homepage; a task or article title should remain recognizable.

Write a description that accurately summarizes the page; do not add unsupported promises or duplicate keyword lists. Set relevant canonical and Open Graph fields when the page is public and shareable. Use an actual absolute share-image URL, supported dimensions, accessible preview text, and the correct production environment. Test the rendered preview when possible; caches may require refresh after deployment.

Alt text describes the image's function or content in context. It is not an SEO keyword container. Use empty alt text for decoration and HTML text for essential information embedded in an image. See part 26 for assets and part 34 for SEO mechanics.

## Banners, consent, and listings

Give temporary announcements a real lifecycle and dismissal behavior where suitable. Consent controls should state purpose and provide meaningful choices. Marketplace listings need real capabilities, pricing, screenshots, and support details. Do not claim certifications or approvals the product does not have.

## Review

Check destinations, sender identity, factual details, consent expectations, environment, and preview behavior. Confirm whether the task is to draft or publish; sending to other people requires appropriate authorization.
