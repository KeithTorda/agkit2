# 01 - Word choice and brand voice

Read when writing visible copy or evaluating whether language communicates anything useful. This is a vocabulary judgment guide, not a list of forbidden words.

## Start with the voice and the reader

Use the audience, language, tone, and references agreed in the design intake. A luxury campaign, a game, a developer tool, and a payment error need different voices. Marketing may be evocative; controls and critical instructions need to be unambiguous. Brand slogans, specialist terms, client wording, and deliberate humor are valid when understandable and truthful.

Words such as "powerful," "seamless," "unlock," "journey," "intuitive," or "premium" are not intrinsically wrong. Ask whether they carry useful meaning here. A short branded headline can create a mood; the supporting content should explain what the product offers. Do not remove personality merely because a word appears frequently in generated copy.

## Make claims assessable

| If the wording is weak because... | Improve it by... |
|---|---|
| "A powerful dashboard" leaves its function unclear | Naming relevant capabilities: "Sales and stock across your branches" |
| "Instant settlement" may promise unavailable behavior | Stating the actual processing window and exceptions |
| "Bank-grade security" has no defined meaning | Describing verified controls in language the audience needs |
| "Best school in the region" is unsupported | Providing a real award with source and date, or a noncomparative promise |
| A feature description repeats its title | Adding a concrete benefit, example, limitation, or workflow |
| "Just enter your ID" minimizes a difficult step | Saying which ID and where to find it |

Retain uncertainty where evidence is incomplete. "Usually arrives within two business days" may be more accurate than an absolute guarantee. Remove filler that adds no meaning, but do not turn justified hedges into false certainty. Distinguish a measured claim from an aspiration or brand statement.

## Choose terms deliberately

- Name the actual action and object where ambiguity matters: "Export receipts" may help more than "Continue." A contextual "Continue" is fine in a clear sequence.
- Keep domain terms consistent. Do not alternate "resident," "customer," and "member" for the same role unless the roles differ.
- Define unfamiliar jargon at its first useful occurrence. Preserve technical terms for technical readers.
- Describe users without unnecessary assumptions about ability, age, gender, income, education, or location.
- Use institution names, roles, and product names as supplied or verified. Do not invent official titles or credentials.
- Preserve user-authored content and names. A stylistic cleanup does not authorize rewriting quoted testimony or changing a person's name.

## Rewrite method

Identify the reader's question; retain the sentence's useful fact or emotion; remove repeated padding; choose concrete language where the task needs precision. Compare the revision against the brand voice and surrounding composition. Read it aloud: mechanical short sentences can be as weak as inflated prose.

## Review

Check unclear terms, unsupported absolutes, inconsistent terminology, accidental exclusion, and promises the product cannot fulfill. Do not use a word-search hit as a failure. The same word can be empty in one context and exact in another.
