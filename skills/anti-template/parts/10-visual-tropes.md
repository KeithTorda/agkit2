# 10 - Visual direction and intentional detail

Read when shaping a page, interpreting visual references, or reviewing a composition. The selected design direction from the user intake is the basis for decisions. A technique does not become a defect because a template or another site also uses it.

## Interpret references before choosing ingredients

Identify what the user likes: typography, palette, imagery, composition, material, density, motion, or overall mood. Combine those qualities with the actual content and product purpose. Do not blindly copy an entire reference, and do not flatten it into a standard white page to avoid resemblance.

Choose a visual concept that fits the subject. Specificity can come from a strong type pairing, a considered image crop, a distinctive grid, a restrained interaction, or expressive material treatment. Originality does not require unusualness in every component.

## Techniques and execution criteria

| Technique | Valid use | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Glass and translucent surfaces | A layered interface, photo-led navigation, a chosen material system | Contrast over actual backdrops, legible fallback, blur cost, forced-color behavior |
| Gradients, mesh, glow, spotlight | Brand atmosphere, a focal accent, expressive technology or entertainment direction | Text contrast, focal hierarchy, responsive cropping, stable fallback |
| Grain, paper, metal, clay, retro texture | Editorial character, tactile branding, game or campaign identity | Readability, asset size, cohesive treatment, no accidental muddy overlay |
| Dark or neon composition | A chosen mood, product context, or audience preference | Color relationships, content hierarchy, visible controls, readable data |
| Bento, equal cards, asymmetry, split composition | A useful grouping or storytelling structure | Content fit, reading order, hierarchy, repetition with purpose |
| Tilted screenshots and device mockups | A launch composition or product narrative | Clear product details, honest representation, perspective that preserves meaning |
| Floating fragments and illustrative UI | A conceptual preview or explanation | Clearly presented as a concept when fictional; meaningful grouping |
| Full-bleed imagery, colored sections, decorative dividers | Narrative pacing or brand expression | Transitions that feel deliberate, sufficient contrast, coherent structure |
| Motion and interactive effects | A selected brand experience, teaching sequence, or feedback | Accessible alternatives, reduced motion, real performance, content availability |

The table is a set of evaluation questions. It is not a requirement to justify each effect in a report.

## Page composition

A centered hero with cards can be exactly right. A table or editorial page can also be right. Start with the visitor's goal, useful content, and agreed style. Repetition can create rhythm and consistency; vary a layout when a new content relationship warrants it. Do not impose a minimum number of layout families or replace every familiar pattern.

Use images, screenshots, illustration, 3D, data, or type alone according to their purpose. No image quota or automatic screenshot preference applies. Use decorative marks when they reinforce the composition; remove them when their position, scale, or quality competes with the intended focal point.

## Component and library use

Keep accessible interaction primitives and adapt their styling to the direction. A component library's defaults can be suitable, especially in an existing product. Customize where the brief calls for it; do not add a library merely to imitate an effect, or reject one because its demos are recognizable. Consider dependency cost, maintainability, and required behavior.

Icons, badges, pricing highlights, testimonial walls, marquees, carousels, and FAQ accordions are available choices. Their content must be truthful and their controls usable. An animation cannot turn invented social proof into real evidence.

## Review by observation

Inspect the actual output at the important sizes. Ask what draws the eye first, whether the content sequence is clear, whether text and imagery feel specific to this brand, and whether the execution matches the supplied references. Repair concrete issues such as weak hierarchy, mismatched assets, inconsistent treatment, unreadable overlays, accidental gaps, or broken states. Do not call a color, font, gradient, card shape, or motion effect an "AI tell" by itself.
