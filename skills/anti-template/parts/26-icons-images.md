# 26 - Icons, images, and visual assets

Read when selecting, creating, or integrating visual assets. Use the user's references, real content, and selected direction. There is no required image count and no automatic preference for generated art, photography, stock, screenshots, or no imagery.

## Select assets by role

An asset can demonstrate a product, establish mood, tell a story, explain a concept, identify a brand, or support scanning. Choose the medium that best serves that role. Relevant licensed stock can be excellent; client photos can be distinctive; generated illustration or 3D can be exactly right for a chosen aesthetic. Type-led designs can work without images.

Avoid irrelevant filler and mismatched quality. Inspect crops, lighting, visual style, text artifacts, anatomy where present, and how the asset relates to the page. Placeholder services or watermarked previews should not silently ship as final content. If an essential asset is missing, use a clearly identified prototype placeholder or a deliberate alternative and report the gap.

## Authenticity and rights

Use assets with appropriate rights and respect consent and attribution requirements. Do not present generated people as real staff, invented testimonials as customers, fictional buildings as actual facilities, or concept screens as a working product. Generated art representing an imaginative world is different from evidence of a real place or person. Label conceptual content when needed to avoid misleading the audience.

A prototype may use fictional data and identities when clearly identified and isolated. A public launch needs real proof where proof is claimed. Do not invent logos, partnerships, accreditation, or ratings.

## Icon systems and emoji

Choose icon shape, weight, color, material, and scale to support the direction. A coherent mix of styles can be intentional, such as filled selected states with outline defaults or custom brand icons with a utility set. Consistency of meaning matters more than a blanket "one set" rule.

Icons may appear in navigation, headings, buttons, forms, features, or data when they add recognition or visual character. There is no per-screen icon quota. Avoid encoding essential status only in an unexplained symbol or color. Icon-only controls need an accessible name, visible discoverability where needed, adequate hit area, and keyboard/touch access.

Emoji can suit a playful brand or campaign. Consider cross-platform rendering and spoken names. Hide purely decorative symbols from assistive technology where appropriate, keep textual status meaning, and use language names rather than relying on flags to represent languages.

## Avatars and logos

User photos, illustrated avatars, initials, and branded defaults can all work. Provide a stable fallback for missing images. When a name is already adjacent, avatar alt text can be empty to avoid repetition; a standalone identity image needs useful context. Presence indicators require real presence data.

Use legitimate logo files and variants with correct proportions, clear space, and readable backgrounds. Do not recolor or distort marks merely to fit a theme without permission. Provide actual favicon/app icon assets when in scope and check that template logos are removed where no longer relevant.

## Treatment and delivery

Gradient overlays, tints, frames, masks, shadows, perspective, and rounded corners are choices. Test actual text contrast, important focal cropping, and image clarity. Text carrying essential meaning should be available in HTML or another accessible form even if stylized in an asset. Do not automatically reject an image because it contains legible generated text; inspect it and correct errors.

Use suitable formats, responsive sources, dimensions or aspect ratio, and loading priorities. Do not lazy-load the likely critical hero/LCP image by reflex. Defer below-fold assets where useful. Use local or external delivery according to rights, stability, caching, and project needs; not every external image URL is a placeholder.

Video can be a key visual. Provide a poster/fallback, appropriate controls, captions/transcript for meaningful content, accessible motion/audio behavior, and measured transfer/performance choices.

## Review

Inspect every final visible asset in context at relevant crops and sizes. Check rights, authenticity, source stability, alt text, icon names, fallback, loading, contrast, and coherence with the selected direction.
