# 02 - Sentences and rhythm

Read when copy sounds formulaic, repetitive, or difficult to scan. Preserve the agreed voice; no punctuation mark, heading shape, slogan structure, or rhetorical device is categorically disallowed.

## Build a useful reading sequence

Lead with what the reader needs at this point. Each sentence should add a fact, instruction, image, feeling, or useful connection. Vary sentence length when it improves flow. Related ideas can share a sentence; a clipped series of fragments is not automatically clearer.

Use headings to organize the actual content. A question works well for a real FAQ or a reader's decision. A descriptive heading works well for reference material. Repeated rhetorical questions become weak when every answer is the same sales pitch.

| Pattern to evaluate | Keep when... | Revise when... |
|---|---|---|
| "Less effort. More adventure." | It fits the campaign and supporting offer | It could describe any product and replaces needed information |
| "Whether you're a student or a professional..." | The distinction explains relevant paths | It creates a long preamble with no different outcome |
| "Not only X, but Y" | The contrast clarifies a real benefit | Every paragraph uses the same formula |
| A three-part slogan | Its rhythm expresses the brand | The words are interchangeable filler |
| An expressive question heading | The question matters to the audience | It delays a straightforward task or answer |
| A short closing invitation | It names the real next step | It repeats a CTA already obvious in context |

## Formatting and punctuation

Choose case, punctuation, emphasis, and emoji according to the brand and medium. Sentence case often scans well in product UI; editorial or display treatments can use title case or capitals. Check letter spacing and readability rather than enforcing a casing ban. Exclamation marks can suit celebration; critical errors should remain precise and calm.

Em dashes, colons, parentheses, and semicolons are tools. Use them when they improve meaning. Avoid repeated mannerisms that dominate the copy. Do not enforce quotas per page or per word count.

Bold a useful phrase rather than whole paragraphs when emphasis needs contrast. Underlining nonlinks can mislead on the web; if the visual direction uses decorative underlines, make interactive links identifiable by their full interaction treatment. Emoji may be appropriate to a playful voice; do not rely on them alone to communicate status or actions.

## Paragraphs and lists

Use paragraphs for connected reasoning or story, lists for parallel items or steps, and tables for comparison. Do not force every section into three bullets. Give lists a meaningful ordering. Avoid repeating a section title as its opening sentence unless the repetition aids orientation.

## Review

Read the page in order and then headings alone. Check that the sequence still makes sense, repetitions are intentional, and the tone remains consistent. Preserve a distinctive line when it serves the direction; repair vague support around it instead of flattening the entire voice.
