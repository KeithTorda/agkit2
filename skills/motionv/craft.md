# Story, timing, type and sound

Use the accepted brief, visual direction and real assets. Good motion has a reason, readable pacing and coherent transitions; it is not measured by scene count, effect count or arbitrary minimum font size.

## A useful timing record

For substantial work, `STORYBOARD.md` can record purpose, audience/destination, format, duration, accepted look, assets and audio approach, followed by scene rows:

| Time | Purpose and exact copy | Visual | Motion/transition | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 s | Identify the real product | Supplied product image and title | Deliberate arrival, then readable hold | Agreed cue or silence |
| 3–8 s | Explain the useful difference | Accurate demonstration | Continuity from the prior composition | Voice/captions when requested |

These times are examples, not a prescription. A small edit can use a simpler change note. Do not invent factual benefits, prices, customers or product UI to fill a storyboard.

## Pacing and choreography

Let the audience recognize the subject and purpose early when the destination calls for it. Give copy enough settled reading time at the actual size and complexity; a word-count heuristic can estimate a draft but does not replace watching it. Technical explanation, names and figures may need longer than a slogan. A title card, slow film and social ad need different pacing.

Establish a focal relationship. Multiple things can move together when choreography communicates hierarchy; one-motion-per-scene is not a universal law. Repeat a movement vocabulary to create identity and change it when meaning changes. Match easing, travel, duration and overlap to the material and action, rather than applying one entrance/stagger recipe everywhere.

Use the engine's deterministic timing. Initial and final states must be correct when seeking directly to any frame, not just after playback from zero. Test transition boundaries, scene overlap and interrupted/embedded playback where relevant. For a loop, inspect continuity of position, velocity, light and audio across the seam; identical first/last frames alone can still produce a visible pause.

## Type, captions and safe areas

Inspect text at the actual final viewing size, with intended crop/destination UI. Choose size, line length, line breaks and hold from the content and composition. Avoid fixed “1080p minimum” recipes that ignore the actual scene and viewing context. Keep relevant glyphs, accents and prices accurate; do not animate operational figures to fabricated values.

Check contrast over the actual brightest/darkest footage and moving backgrounds. A scrim, outline, band, repositioning or controlled crop can preserve the chosen art direction. Keep essential information away from actual platform overlays and crop regions; verify current destination guidance rather than relying on hardcoded pixels for every vertical platform.

Provide captions/transcript/description when relevant to the content and audience. Burned-in captions, sidecar subtitles and selectable player captions serve different destinations; do not force all videos to include a music bed, voice or burned text. Time captions to the actual speech and break at natural phrases. Match the requested language/register and inspect transcription, names and numbers.

## Sound

Mix for intelligibility and appropriate destination loudness. Measure the actual audio rather than treating a universal -14 LUFS or -18 dB bed as guaranteed correct. Voice, music and SFX need deliberate balance, headroom, fades and ducking where useful. A silent piece can be intentional.

Use supplied/licensed media or available authorized generation. Check actual language/voice capability instead of repeating a dated provider-language limit. Keep source/provenance and required terms with the project. Playback review is needed for sync, clipping, clicks, level jumps, timing and start/end behavior; a contact sheet cannot verify sound.

## Delivery review

Inspect important frames, transitions and final output. Recheck text, crops, seams, audio and metadata after the final render rather than assuming it matches the draft. Report the actual reviewed portion, format and limitations; do not call every unseen frame flawless because automated validation passed.
